CO–PO mapping: UGC LOCF Mathematics graduate attributes

Which programme outcomes each course outcome serves, on the 1–3 scale of NAAC and NBA matrices (1 slight, 2 moderate, 3 substantial). An outcome serves a PO when its verbs, the skills it names or its words match the PO's indicators; it maps more strongly when indicators of two kinds match, and when its Bloom level reaches the level the PO calls for. A course's cell is the rounded mean over its outcomes that serve the PO. Source: UGC Learning Outcomes-based Curriculum Framework, B.A./B.Sc. (Honours) Mathematics, section 3.

How this was worked out

Every course title and topic line was searched for the terms listed under each skill in the Undergraduate Mathematics taxonomy (48 skills). Course outcomes are not counted as evidence of a skill: they often look ahead to later study, and not every syllabus states them. Matching ignores case and punctuation, and the longest phrase wins (“partial differential equations” counts for PDEs, not ODEs). No AI or statistics are involved: each mark in this report traces back to a phrase in a syllabus.

Limits: a syllabus can teach a skill without naming it, and naming it does not show depth. Treat a gap as a question for the committee, not a verdict. Peer findings are only as good as the peer syllabi supplied.

Core and electives: a skill counts as core for a programme when its compulsory courses alone cover it, and as elective only when it depends on courses a student may choose. Gaps are judged on everything a programme offers; the split shows how firmly each peer teaches it.

Courses on a skill: a course counts as on a skill when its title names the skill, or when the skill appears in at least half of its topic lines (and in at least three). A programme without one teaches the skill inside courses about something else, which may mean a few examples rather than a unit of study.
